# The line beside the domain

## Short answer

The most valuable line is not a slogan. It is the descriptor people actually type into Google, placed immediately after the name in the title tag, and repeated identically on every profile. For Roni Paul that descriptor is **health-tech entrepreneur**, not "serial entrepreneur".

## Why "health-tech entrepreneur" wins

- **People search categories, not compliments.** "Health tech entrepreneur India", "healthtech founder Bengaluru" and "pharmacy tech founder" are real query patterns. Almost nobody searches "serial entrepreneur Bengaluru".
- **Google's knowledge panel wants a short, verifiable descriptor**, of the form "Indian entrepreneur" or "Founder of MedMate". It pulls that from the site's schema and from consistent third-party profiles. "India's Health Tech Entrepreneur" reads as a claim, and a superlative-sounding title can be ignored or treated as promotional.
- **Keep "serial entrepreneur" as a supporting phrase** in the description and bio. It is credibility, not discovery.

## Three rules that matter more than the wording

1. **One descriptor, everywhere.** Google links the entity by matching the same phrase across the website, LinkedIn, Instagram, Crunchbase and any press coverage. Mixing "health-tech founder", "serial entrepreneur" and "pharmacy tech leader" across profiles dilutes it.
2. **Name first, always.** The title tag should start with "Roni Paul", because the name is the query to win first and the anchor Google builds the knowledge panel around.
3. **Say "Indian" or "Bengaluru", not "India's".** "Indian health-tech entrepreneur" is how Wikipedia and knowledge panels phrase nationality, and it matches how journalists write. "India's" implies a ranking that cannot be proven.

## Checklist to apply

- Title tag, meta description and Open Graph title on ronipaul.com
- Person schema: jobTitle and description
- Share card kicker line
- Hero kicker on the page: "Health-tech entrepreneur" as the first, static descriptor
- The three copyable bios in the press kit
- LinkedIn headline, Instagram bio and both profiles' website fields, updated the same day

## Where category ranking actually comes from

The title tag gets the page found for the name within weeks. Ranking for "health-tech entrepreneur" as a category takes months of essays that use the phrase naturally in headings and body text, plus a few outlets linking to the page with that phrase as the anchor text.
